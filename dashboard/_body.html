Buy, refurbish and sell

Buys at your purchase price from the deal below, or, if you haven't given one, at the model's value for the condition the works put right; sells at the value after them, both from the repeat-sales calibration. Works are Yorkshire guide prices plus a contingency (sources in Method). A kitchen and a rewire grow only with the square root of floor area, each bathroom and the boiler are fixed amounts, and decorating, flooring, plastering and radiators go by floor area; kitchens and bathrooms are cheaper in cheaper homes unless you pick a finish. Finance is a bridging loan on part of the price: interest on the loan and an arrangement fee; the rest of the price, the works and the buying costs are your cash. Set the loan to 0% for a cash purchase.

        Against the usual quick methods

        Median error on the same 2025-26 sales, each method using only sales at least two months old (by then most sales are published, though new builds and some others take months longer). Index methods need a previous sale, so they cover 72% of homes. The hedonic regression is the fair baseline: a linear model on this model's own features, with a fixed effect for each postcode sector, trained and tested the same way.

        Year by year

        Each year valued by a model trained on sales up to two years before, with a price index rebuilt as it would have been known at the time.

        A strict timing check

        The published test lets a sale take its size, rooms and build details from a certificate lodged up to 90 days after it, or from the home's first certificate if none came before. Here only certificates lodged by the sale date count. The 2025 deprivation scores and plot sizes from today's title plans have no dated versions, so the third row drops them altogether. That is a sensitivity test, not a measure of their timing: it also loses what they legitimately add. Each row is the average of three runs with different random seeds; the change is paired with the published rules seed by seed.

        Where it is more and less accurate

        Test sales, by group. Land Registry registers some sales months late, new builds most of all: they are 4% of the 2025 test sales and none from February 2026, and August 2026 has 82 sales against a usual 300 to 400. New builds are the easiest homes to value, so the lag doesn't flatter the 2026 part of the test: January to August 2026 scores much the same as the same months of 2025.         How the condition setting was calibrated

        Homes resold within 3 to 18 months are mostly bought, refurbished and resold. Against a model without last-sale information, they bought 7.6% below the estimate and sold 7.7% above it. Homes resold after 5 years or more also sold about 1.4% above it, at both ends, for reasons that aren't a quick refurbishment, so that is taken off. Each home's sales are kept in the same fold, so the model never learns a home's price from its own other sale. With a last sale entered the full model already knows part of it: a purchase in need of renovation then sits 4.7% below (on the 546 purchases that had an earlier sale, a median 10 years before), and a quick resale only 0.6% above, so those figures apply when the last sale is known (for "good" and "recently refurbished" only when it was within 18 months; works since an older sale aren't visible to the model, and keep the first column).

        How a value is made

        Every step only uses information that existed before the sale being valued.

        1. Sales and certificates50,042 Land Registry sales since 2015, 94% matched to their energy certificate (size, rooms, age, energy, walls, heating, certificate history).
        2. Location and prices nearbyRecent prices on the street, in the postcode, sector and neighbourhood; the 10 nearest sales; plot size from title plans; deprivation.
        3. The home's last saleWhen known, its previous price moved to today with the index. It's one of the strongest inputs.
        4. The modelLightGBM, 2,043 trees, predicting price relative to a monthly quality-adjusted Doncaster index. It runs here in the page.
        5. Local correctionHalf of the model's median miss on the 40 nearest sales of the last 12 months, for areas moving faster or slower than Doncaster.
        6. Range and confidenceAn 80% range and a typical error for this home, from the model's errors on years it hadn't seen.

        Data

        • Sales: HM Land Registry Price Paid Data for Doncaster, standard sales, 1995 to August 2026.
        • Energy certificates: 130,780 Doncaster certificates (2012 to September 2026), matched on postcode plus the exact house and flat numbers. The energy rating only comes from a certificate lodged before the sale, so later work can't leak in. Size, rooms and build details can come from a certificate lodged after the sale when there is no earlier one; the strict check in Model accuracy finds little change overall across three seeds, though the few homes affected are valued less well without them. Neighbour price per m² still uses each neighbour's best-known floor area.
        • Location: postcodes.io coordinates and 2021 neighbourhoods; English Indices of Deprivation 2025.
        • Plot: HM Land Registry title plans with OS Open UPRN address points.
        • Rents: ONS Price Index of Private Rents and DWP Local Housing Allowance 2026-27.
        • Around this home: Environment Agency flood risk, NaPTAN, DfE and Ofsted, OpenStreetMap, ONS urban/rural class.
        • Works costs: extensions £1,350-£2,200/m² single-storey and £1,200-£2,000/m² two-storey in Yorkshire before VAT and fees (less.co.uk, 2026); rear-dormer lofts £2,200-£2,800/m² (planwatch.co.uk, 2026). Refurbishment at less.co.uk's Yorkshire prices (April 2026), with their 3-bed prices taken at Doncaster's typical 3-bed semi (84 m², from the certificates): a kitchen £5,900 budget or £10,000 mid-range, growing with the square root of floor area (the London Plan's kitchen standards grow at 0.42-0.58 of floor area); a bathroom £3,200 or £5,800 each; a rewire £5,940, also by the square root (from the circuits a 3-bed and 5-bed need); a £1,950 boiler plus £26/m² of radiators; decorating £37/m², flooring £32/m² and re-skimming £30/m². Unless a finish is picked, kitchens and bathrooms run from the bottom of the budget range (£4,550, £2,300) at Doncaster's 10th-percentile price (£100,000) through budget at the median (£180,000) to mid-range at the 90th percentile (£340,000), an assumption. Plus the Federation of Master Builders' 10% contingency.

        Testing

        • Split by time: trained 2015-2023, tuned on 2024, tested on January 2025 to August 2026. The year-by-year backtest repeats this for every year since 2020.
        • No look-ahead: the index for each test month only uses sales at least two months older. Limiting the local features the same way moves the result by 0.03 points.
        • Fair comparisons: every change was fitted with 3 random seeds; smaller than the seed spread counts as noise.
        • Ranges: scaled on 2024 errors and checked on 2025-26.
        • Clean-up: 224 training sales (0.5%) more than 2.2 times from the estimate were removed as likely non-market sales; for sales from January 2025 the same threshold is applied against the test model's estimate (14 of 2,757 removed).
        • The model on this page: after testing, refitted on 46,566 kept sales from January 2015 to August 2025; the last 12 months drive the local correction.
        • Explanations: the page's SHAP values match LightGBM's to 9 decimal places, and its estimates match to within 0.00000004 on the log scale.

        Tried and left out

        • Flood risk, transport, schools and Ofsted, OpenStreetMap places, urban/rural class: none beat seed-to-seed noise, as nearby prices already reflect them.
        • Exact address positions, similar-size neighbours, the council tax band mix, building outlines: nothing on top of what's in.
        • The home's "premium" at its last sale: 0.1 points worse, as the last price already carries it.
        • Learning time inside the model instead of using a price index, as Zillow does: 0.8 points worse, because trees can't run ahead of the years they were trained on. Time features alongside the index: no better.
        • Correcting or stretching estimates by their size to remove the price-level bias: no better, or 0.7 points worse.
        • A smoothed local correction, weighting every recent sale by distance, age and type: no better than the 40 nearest.
        • Forcing more floor area to never lower the value: needs a different loss, about 0.1 points worse.

        Limitations

        • The model can't see condition, layout, views or parking; the condition setting covers the first.
        • Rents are Doncaster-wide averages, so area yields are indicative.
        • Investment defaults (fees, maintenance, works, mortgage rate) are estimates to replace with quotes; tax is simplified.
        • The council tax check compares ranks, not prices, and isn't an input to the estimate.
        • Flood, transport, places and Ofsted grades are 2025-26 snapshots; the OpenStreetMap extract stops at the South Yorkshire border.
        • Addresses carry Royal Mail and Ordnance Survey rights; keep this dashboard for personal use.
